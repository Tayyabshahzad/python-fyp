fig9_common.py — the width and normalisation constants, with the full record
9.py — assembles all four panels · 9_1.py … 9_4.py — one panel each, for faster testing

Each level is drawn as a blurry blob of width Γ. The graph counts how many blobs sit at the Fermi energy: blobs apart → big wiggles, blobs overlapping → flat line.

Known issue. The paper's own Γ = 0.1√B does not reproduce the paper's own figure — its dips stop at 0.53 instead of reaching zero. And our wiggles run the wrong way round: small at high field where the paper's are large. The baseline height is Γ ÷ level spacing, and the spacing grows like B, so Γ ∝ √B gives growing wiggles on a sloping baseline while Γ ∝ B gives a flat baseline with constant wiggles. The published panel has both. Twenty-five width settings, two divisors and the level cutoff were all tested; the trend never reverses. Documented rather than fitted around.

The caption's ρ₀ cannot serve both curves. It defines ρ₀ = A⁻¹ × 10⁻³⁵, but σ_xy carries no factor of A while σ_xx carries one — so ρ_xy/ρ₀ is linear in A and ρ_xx/ρ₀ is quadratic. Fitting A so ρ_xy at 2 T matches the published 0.0022 gives A = 3.3×10⁻⁴⁰, and that same A predicts a ρ_xx peak of 1.5×10⁻³⁴ against the published 0.005 — out by 32 orders of magnitude. Each curve therefore gets its own display constant, both anchored to a published value.
